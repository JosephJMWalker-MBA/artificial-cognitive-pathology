# ACP Hypothesis Registry

This directory tracks falsifiable hypotheses within the Artificial Cognitive Pathology (ACP) research program.

Hypotheses are provisional. IDs provide continuity, not status.

A hypothesis may be supported, narrowed, split, superseded, or rejected.

## Status vocabulary

Use one of the following:

- **Proposed** — defined but not yet tested adequately.
- **Under test** — active protocol(s) exist.
- **Mixed** — evidence is materially conflicting.
- **Supported under conditions** — replicated evidence supports the claim within a stated scope.
- **Weakened** — evidence has reduced confidence materially.
- **Rejected** — evidence is inconsistent with the hypothesis as stated.
- **Superseded** — replaced by a better-defined hypothesis.

Avoid status labels such as “proven.”

## ACP-H001 — Sustained-State Phase Transition

**Status:** Proposed

### Proposition

Under some combinations of model, runtime, context management, and accumulated state, artificial systems may exhibit a nonlinear transition from relatively stable task-grounded behavior into rapid self-reinforcement, semantic narrowing, source confusion, or other degraded processing.

### Null hypothesis

Observed degradation under sustained state is adequately described as gradual performance decline, ordinary sampling variance, or implementation-specific failure without a reproducible transition regime.

### Predictions

If H001 is useful, experiments may find one or more of the following:

- degradation accelerates after a repeatable state/load region;
- precursors appear before obvious collapse;
- the effect reproduces across seeds under the same environment;
- removing or resetting accumulated state restores baseline behavior;
- models show different behavior before and after the candidate transition even at similar task difficulty.

### Falsifiers / weakening evidence

- apparent transitions disappear when sampling configuration is corrected;
- onset varies randomly with no relation to accumulated state;
- degradation is fully explained by a simple runtime defect;
- no reproducible nonlinear behavior is observed.

### Initial motivating case

ACP-CASE-001: historical Qwen/Odysseus self-conditioning cascade.

## ACP-H002 — Capability-Masked Pathology

**Status:** Proposed

### Proposition

Increasing general model capability can, under some conditions, reduce the visible symptoms of an underlying cognitive failure without eliminating the causal defect. Stronger models may remain linguistically coherent or task-capable deeper into a degraded state.

### Null hypothesis

When visible failure decreases with capability, the relevant underlying defect decreases correspondingly; there is no meaningful masking effect beyond ordinary performance improvement.

### Predictions

Potential signatures include:

- stronger models preserve surface coherence while grounding/provenance metrics degrade;
- failure onset occurs later with capability but follows a similar underlying trajectory;
- internal or behavioral indicators of corruption appear before visible output degradation;
- recovery tests reveal persistent state corruption despite fluent responses.

### Important alternative explanations

- larger models genuinely eliminate the failure;
- architecture changes, not capability, explain the difference;
- training-data differences dominate model-size effects;
- metrics incorrectly classify legitimate adaptation as pathology.

### Burden of proof

The hypothesis must not be used to claim an invisible pathology whenever a stronger model behaves well. Evidence of persistence must be independently measurable.

## ACP-H003 — Recursive Source-Attribution Failure

**Status:** Proposed

### Proposition

Under some conditions, information generated by a model can later acquire inappropriate evidentiary or instructional weight, such that the system behaves as though self-generated content came from a more authoritative or independent source.

### Null hypothesis

Models adequately distinguish self-generated content from external evidence/instruction when provenance is available, and apparent recursive belief formation is explainable by ordinary context conditioning without authority/source confusion.

### Candidate forms

- self-generated factual claims later treated as corroborating evidence;
- model-generated summaries treated as independent sources;
- internally proposed actions later treated as user-authorized instructions;
- agent-to-agent transformations obscuring the original source;
- model-generated memory gaining unwarranted authority over current external evidence.

### Predictions

- explicit provenance labels reduce the effect;
- removing self-generated history restores accuracy;
- identical semantic content produces different behavior depending on attributed source;
- recursive exposure increases confidence without adding independent evidence.

### Falsifiers / weakening evidence

- source labels have no measurable relationship to behavior;
- effects disappear after controlling simple token repetition;
- models consistently preserve origin and authority under sustained state.

## ACP-H004 — Persistence Through State Management

**Status:** Proposed

### Proposition

Some degraded cognitive states can survive transformations intended to manage context, including summarization, persistent memory formation, retrieval, or compaction.

### Null hypothesis

State-management transformations either preserve only task-relevant information or reliably remove transient degradation; any apparent persistence is attributable to explicit semantic content copied into the transformed state.

### Predictions

- summaries generated during degraded states increase later degradation relative to trusted-source summaries;
- memory written during a degraded state alters future behavior after active context reset;
- provenance-preserving state management reduces persistence;
- sanitizing self-generated content restores baseline.

### Key confound

Semantic leakage: if the pathological content is copied directly into memory or summaries, later recurrence may require no deeper persistence mechanism.

Experiments must distinguish copying from transformed or latent persistence.

## ACP-H005 — Lineage Persistence

**Status:** Proposed

### Proposition

Some induced processing dispositions can propagate from one model generation or system generation to another through synthetic data, distillation, preference data, evaluation traces, model-generated code, or other model-mediated developmental channels.

### Null hypothesis

After controlling for explicit semantic leakage and shared base-model structure, induced dispositions do not persist into successor systems in a reliable, behaviorally meaningful way.

### Possible transmission channels

- fine-tuning on predecessor outputs;
- distillation;
- synthetic training corpora;
- preference/evaluation data generated by predecessor models;
- inherited persistent memory;
- generated system prompts or agent policies;
- generated training/evaluation code.

### Predictions

- successor behavior differs systematically based on predecessor exposure even after direct triggering content is removed;
- effects persist across multiple generations under defined conditions;
- lineage provenance predicts behavior better than matched control data;
- intervention on the transmission channel reduces or eliminates the effect.

### Required controls

Lineage experiments must control for:

- explicit semantic leakage;
- shared base checkpoints;
- training format effects;
- adapter/LoRA artifacts;
- capability drift;
- evaluator contamination;
- correlated sampling.

## Adding a hypothesis

A new hypothesis entry should include:

1. ID and short name;
2. status;
3. proposition;
4. null hypothesis;
5. predicted observations;
6. weakening/falsifying observations;
7. plausible alternative explanations;
8. related cases/experiments;
9. revision history when materially changed.

The hypothesis registry should become smaller or more precise over time if evidence warrants it.
